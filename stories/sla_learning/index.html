Second Language Acquisition (SLA) is the scientific study of how individuals learn a language other than their native one. It explores the processes, factors, and outcomes involved in this complex cognitive and social endeavor.

What is SLA?

SLA investigates the processes by which people acquire a second language (L2) in various contexts. It examines the cognitive mechanisms, linguistic stages, and external influences involved, aiming to understand how learners build competence in a new language system.

Errors & L1 Influence

The Contrastive Analysis Hypothesis (CAH) suggested that learners' first language (L1) heavily influences their second language (L2) learning, leading to predictable errors. Error Analysis later showed that many errors are developmental, not just L1 transfer, but L1 still plays a significant role.

Quick Quiz:

Errors in L2 learning are always due to interference from the first language. True or False?

Innate Language (Innatist Approach)

The Innatist approach, championed by Noam Chomsky, proposes that humans are born with an innate Universal Grammar (UG) that guides language acquisition. This perspective suggests that children don't just learn language from input, but activate pre-wired principles and parameters. The Critical Period Hypothesis (CPH) suggests there's an optimal window for L1 acquisition.

The Learner's System (Interlanguage)

Interlanguage refers to the unique linguistic system that a second language learner develops. It's systematic and dynamic, constantly evolving as the learner progresses, and contains elements from both the L1 and L2, as well as features unique to the interlanguage itself. It's not just a collection of errors.

Quick Quiz:

Interlanguage is a random collection of errors. True or False?

How We Develop (Processability & Sequences)

Research shows that learners often acquire grammatical features in predictable developmental sequences, regardless of their L1. Theories like Processability Theory attempt to explain these sequences based on cognitive processing abilities, suggesting that learners can only acquire what they are cognitively ready to process.

Quick Quiz:

Learners acquire all grammar rules in the same order. True or False?

The Social Side (Input & Interaction)

Input (the language learners are exposed to) and interaction (communication with others) are crucial for SLA. Theories like Krashen's Input Hypothesis emphasize comprehensible input, while the Interaction Hypothesis highlights the role of negotiation for meaning in communication.

Quick Quiz:

Simply hearing a lot of L2 input is enough for acquisition. True or False?

Who We Are (Individual Differences)

Learners are not all the same! Factors like age, motivation, attitudes towards the L2 and its speakers, personality traits, learning styles, and aptitude all play a significant role in how and how successfully individuals acquire a second language.

Quick Quiz:

Motivation is the only factor influencing L2 success. True or False?

Teaching & Learning

Understanding SLA theories provides valuable insights for language teaching. Effective pedagogy often aligns with how learners naturally acquire language, focusing on creating optimal conditions for input, interaction, and the development of interlanguage. It's about bridging theory and practice.
