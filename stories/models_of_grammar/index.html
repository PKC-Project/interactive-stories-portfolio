Introduction: Theories and Models

What exactly is a "model of grammar"? It's a formal system designed to describe and explain the structure of language. These models aim to capture how language works, from the smallest units to complex sentences, often with different theoretical assumptions and goals.

Syntax: General Discussion

Syntax is the study of how words combine to form phrases, clauses, and sentences. It's about the rules governing sentence structure. Different models of grammar propose various ways to represent these rules, often using tree diagrams or dependency relations.

Lexical Functional Grammar (LFG)

LFG is a non-transformational theory of grammar that emphasizes the parallel representation of different levels of linguistic structure. It proposes two main structures: c-structure (syntactic categories) and f-structure (grammatical functions). These are linked by lexical entries.

Morphology

Morphology is the study of word structure and how words are formed from smaller units called morphemes. Different grammatical models account for morphological processes (like inflection and derivation) in various ways, either within the lexicon or through separate morphological components.

Optimality Theory (OT)

Optimality Theory (OT) is a constraint-based approach to grammar, particularly prominent in phonology. It proposes that observed linguistic forms arise from the optimal satisfaction of a set of universal, violable, and ranked constraints. The "best" output is the one that violates high-ranked constraints least.

Dependency Grammar (DG)

Dependency Grammar (DG) is a grammatical framework that focuses on direct relationships between words, where one word (the head) governs or "depends" on another (the dependent). Unlike phrase-structure grammars, it does not rely on phrase nodes, instead building structures based on direct word-to-word links.

Construction Grammar (CxG)

Construction Grammar (CxG) views grammatical constructions (e.g., "the X-er the Y-er" or "S V O") as the basic units of language. These constructions are form-meaning pairings, ranging from words to complex sentence patterns, and are learned directly from linguistic experience.

Principles & Parameters (P&P)

Principles and Parameters (P&P) is a generative framework, often associated with Noam Chomsky. It proposes that Universal Grammar consists of innate, universal principles common to all languages, along with a set of parameters that can be set differently for each language (e.g., the "pro-drop" parameter determining if subjects can be dropped).
