The Spark of Inquiry

In 1896, Henri Becquerel discovered mysterious rays emanating from uranium. Intrigued, Polish physicist Marie Skłodowska Curie decided to investigate these "uranium rays" further for her doctoral thesis, suspecting other substances might also be radioactive. Her primary source material was pitchblende.

The Laborious Process

Marie, joined by her husband Pierre, embarked on the monumental task of isolating the radioactive elements from tons of pitchblende. It was grueling work in a basic laboratory, involving countless chemical separations. Their perseverance was extraordinary. Each click simulates processing a batch!

Radiant Discoveries

Their tireless efforts led to the discovery of two new elements: Polonium (named after Marie's homeland) and Radium. Marie Curie became the first woman to win a Nobel Prize, and later the only person to win Nobel Prizes in two different scientific fields (Physics and Chemistry). Her work laid the foundation for nuclear physics and cancer treatments.
