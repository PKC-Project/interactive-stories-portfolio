The Abstract Machine

Before computers were circuits, they were an idea. In 1936, a young Alan Turing imagined a machine... not of gears, but of pure logic. An infinite tape, a simple head. Read, write, move. With these rules, it could compute anything computable.

The Codebreakers' War

This abstract idea became a weapon. During WWII, Turing's insights were key to designing the electromechanical "Bombe" machines that sought patterns in chaos, breaking the 'unbreakable' Enigma code and turning the tide of the war.

The Imitation Game

Having proven a machine could calculate, Turing asked a more profound question: Can a machine think? His "Imitation Game," now the Turing Test, became the foundational thought experiment of artificial intelligence.

The Universal Machine

Turing's vision of a single machine that could perform any task if given the right instructions is the ancestor of every computer, every phone... and the code that is bringing this story to life for you right now.
