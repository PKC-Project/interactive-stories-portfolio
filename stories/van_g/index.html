Van Gogh: The Early Years

Vincent van Gogh was born in Groot Zundert, Netherlands, in 1853. His early life was marked by a search for purpose, leading him through various vocations including art dealing and missionary work. These experiences, often filled with emotional intensity and a deep empathy for the working class, profoundly shaped his worldview before he fully dedicated himself to becoming an artist around 1880. His early works were characterized by somber tones and depicted peasant life.

Arles: Sunflowers & Starry Nights

In 1888, Van Gogh moved to Arles in the South of France, seeking brilliant light and color. This period was one of immense creativity and artistic development, where he produced some of his most iconic masterpieces, including the vibrant "Sunflowers" series and the swirling, celestial "Starry Night." His unique style, characterized by bold colors and expressive, impasto brushwork, began to fully emerge, though his life remained tumultuous.

Legacy and Enduring Impact

Tragically, Vincent van Gogh's life was cut short in 1890. During his lifetime, he achieved little commercial success or recognition. However, his profound emotional honesty, innovative use of color, and dynamic brushwork would posthumously earn him a place as one of an most influential figures in the history of Western art. His work continues to inspire and move millions around the globe.
